# The best free website blocker for Chrome in 2026 (and how to choose one)

_Disclosure: we make FocusFence, one of the blockers mentioned below. We've tried to keep this guide useful even if you pick something else. Facts about other tools were checked against public listings and reviews in November 2026. Features change, so double-check before you install._

Most "best website blocker" lists rank tools by feature count. Feature count isn't what decides whether a blocker works for you. What decides it is whether **you stop bypassing it**. Here is what to look for, and how the main free options compare.

## 1. Does blocking a site also block its subdomains?

If you block `reddit.com`, you want `old.reddit.com`, `m.reddit.com` and `www.reddit.com` gone too. Otherwise the habit just moves one address over. Before you commit to a blocker, test this.

## 2. Can it block *part* of a site?

This is the feature that matters most and gets mentioned least. You might need YouTube for a tutorial but not YouTube Shorts. You might need one subreddit but not the front page. A blocker that only works on whole domains forces a bad choice: lose the useful part, or keep the feed. Look for **path rules** (e.g. `youtube.com/shorts`). On sites like YouTube, check that they also catch in-app navigation, not just links you type.

## 3. Schedules and focus sessions

Blocking all day, every day tends to end in rebellion. Blocking during **your** work hours, plus short focus sprints (Pomodoro: 25 minutes on, 5 off), is easier to live with.

## 4. How hard is it to cheat?

Every browser extension can be disabled from `chrome://extensions`. No extension can prevent that, and any blocker that claims otherwise is overselling. What a good blocker can do is add **friction**: a waiting period and a typing challenge before you can turn it off, and timers that don't quietly run out while it's disabled. If you need a hard lock across your whole computer, a desktop app is the right tool, not an extension.

## 5. Privacy

A website blocker sits next to everything you browse. Prefer blockers that:

- don't require an account,
- don't send your browsing or blocklist to a server,
- use Chrome's built-in blocking engine (declarativeNetRequest) instead of reading every page,
- ask for site access **only for the sites you block**.
